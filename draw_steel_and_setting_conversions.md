A catch-all document containing any differences in interpretation I have from the setting as written in The Delver's Guide to Beastworld, any homebrew rules, and some conversion notes for translating D&D concepts into Draw Steel.

# Ancestries

## Brethren
Brethren are the creatures that come from a parallel world entitled The Broken World. Setting as-written Brethren are 100% human and nothing else. I find that rather limiting so I've expanded Brethren to allow any non-beast ancestry. Their origin and culture remains unchanged and are still assumed to be a largely homogenous people whos cultural and ancestral ties have long since been forgotten.

# Classes

Draw Steel classes are altogether different from your standard D&D setting. There are one or two things to know about specific classes and their interaction with the Beast World.
## Paladin
Paladin is more of a title than a class in the Beast World. Associated with Dramphine, the Moon Wolf, goddess of justice. Paladins are considered the final word when it comes to matter of law in all Homelands but Oria. This title is impossible to obtain in the Beast World without action as proof of value. Notably a player character is not a paladin until they achieve a certain amount of Renown, and only Renown in which they have acted not only good, but lawful. Those that earn the title are generally Conduits or Censors.

## Psionics and Magic
The three magic sources of the Beast World are: Arcana, Divine, and Nature.

The Divine is a classic stance on D&D magic. Gods exist and grant power. With the exception that high level divine casters have a tendency to move past the need to actually pray to their deity for spells. By the time you're high level you embody your god's ideals enough to be a power source in its own right. Of course you can cast this spell, it is what your god would have wanted.

Nature and the Arcana are tightly bound together like a mind to a body. Nature is all the automatic processes of existence, cause, and effect. Arcana is a singular entity bound to Nature that observes and reacts to what it sees. Anything unexplainable by Nature's laws is attributed to the Arcana. This is magic. There are multiple ways to interact with Nature and Arcana to create magic. In D&D terms, druidic and ranger spells are expressed as an invitation for Nature to react. Wizards spend their lives finding loopholes in the overlap of Arcana's will and Nature's laws. Bards spend their time attempting to evoke emotional responses in the Arcana to a specific end. Sorcerers express their inherent existence, strongarming their very will against the Arcana until it submits.

Draw Steel doesn't have these same specific classes. They also have purely psionic flavored classes which the Beast World doesn't include. So everything has to fit somewhere. Conduits and Censors are obviously Divine. Elementalist and Talent could be any flavor of arcane though I personally favor Talents as Sorcerers and Elementalists as either Druids or Wizards. Troubadours have an obvious direct mapping to Bard.

Nulls definitely map to Monk. To change their psionic flavor I'll fall back on Beast World canon. Monks are similar to Sorcerers in that they eventually reach a state of heightened existence that both Nature and Arcana are obligated to reckon with. Though where Sorcerers are born with this feeling that they then exercise through force of will, Monks find their place in the world through meditation and discipline.

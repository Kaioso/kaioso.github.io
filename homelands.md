# Al'ar

An island nation composed almost entirely of cats. Very Caribbean adjacent. If your character is an islander, pirate, seafarer, merchant, or you want to explore such themes you would do well to have them come from this homeland. This homeland also has the distinction of being the most remote of the homelands.

- Dominant ancestries: Feline and brethren
- Cultural and naming touchstones: Caribbean (Post colonial Portuguese, Carib, Indian)
- Tropical islands
- Natural non-stone buildings, tents, hammocks
- Mostly foraging
- Sparse housing preferring to live outside
- Heavy boat life
- Migratory due to foraging
- Used to a cycle of short bursts of hard work followed by leisure
- Population likes their alone time
- Philosophy
- Marriage isn't important
- Child-rearing is matriarchical
- Don't care for wealth unless they can wear it, and wear it they do. Pirates, but kind of sponsored by the country
- Paint and color
- Loose laws with no death penalty.

# Allemance

The wolf kingdom of Allemance. A pastoral Euro-Feudal society. Ruled by their queen, a wolf from a long line of royalty. A standard D&D character from a standard D&D setting would feel right at home here. This is also where Patrie, the Brethren capital, was transplanted to from the Broken World they came from. ALL Brethren are naturally from Allemance as a consequence - Whether they've moved away in the last 15 years since the Pilgrimage or not.

- Dominant ancestries: Canine and bovine
- Cultural and naming touchstones: French, Irish
- Pastoral
- Euro-Feudal
- Culture of unscheduled prioritization. Work is a priority list, not a schedule.
- Community driven, where family and community blend together.
- Culture of free love.
- People are generally flighty and frivolous.

# Arneria

A land literally split in half. The jungles of the Bat'yan taking up one half, and the desert Beylik taking the rest. Each side is ruled by its own separate government structure and sporting its own culture. The Bat'yan covers the mingling themes of jungle heavy nations of the real world such as the Amazon, Congo, and Southeast Asia. The Beylik covers themes of the middle east - Very much your Arabian Nights style fantasy. The Beylik also holds the largest school of magic in the Beast World. If you're a fox and not from Vinyot, chances are you're from the Beylik.

- Dominant ancestries: Murine, Ligonine, Bovine, and Vulpine
- Desert and jungle
- Passionate people
- Dignity and charity
- Biggest military
- Biggest magic school
- Welcoming for tourists but insular when it comes to government meddling of other homelands.

## Bat'yan
- Cultural and naming touchstones: Filipino
- Close and rural
- There's a village leader and a leader of village leaders that can psychically commune
- Couples remain separate in friends and activity based on their gender
- Alternating gender roles based on morning or evening
- Kids stay at home until married
- Flexible and meandering work schedules

## Beylik
- Cultural and naming touchstones: 1600 Ottoman Empire. Turkish and Armenian
- Ingenuity and hard work
- Heavy planning
- Math
- Intellectual pursuits
- College
- Thriving gem trade
- Fast friendship
- Hospitality
- Emphasis on providing for family
- Has a hereditary ruling class

# Oria

The northern homeland of Oria covers themes typical of the frozen north fantasy. Loosely organized into small tight-knit villages - Often all living under one roof. The ruling class is composed almost exclusively of bears and elk.

- Dominant ancestries: Ursine and Cervine
- Cultural and naming touchstones: Russian, Norwegian
- Alpine
- Lodge houses
- Stubborn survival and work ethic to match
- Traditional
- Passing of knowledge, morality
- Children are culturally expected
- Mining and metal craft.

# Vinyot

South of Allemance is Vinyot, the trade center of the Beast World. Covers themes of Italy and the mediterranean. Vinyot was hit hardest by the Invader War 15 years ago as the invasion center.

- Dominant ancestries: Vulpine and brethren
- Cultural and naming touchstones: Italy
- Port cities
- Maritime
- Trade
- Lively conversation and debate
- Invention and innovation
- Forward thinking
- Gambling
- Individualism while espousing the virtues of helping others
- An expected respect for elders
- Marriage is contractual and the parents must also agree
- Bards as part of the Pirhouan religion
- Marriage by classism
- Criminal Justice through debt
- Lawyers
- Racism against tenebrines (racoons, possums)
